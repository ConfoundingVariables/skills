# Research protocol

Research before drafting each new quiz, especially Hard and Expert.

## 1. Anchor to the topic's authoritative scope

Check current authoritative material for the topic's scope, objectives, terminology, or linked reference material relevant to the supplied quiz — a certification's official page, a product's official documentation, or the field's standard references. When an exam profile applies (see SKILL.md), use its named anchors as the scope authority.

## 2. Verify the technical topic

Use authoritative technical sources appropriate to the subject, such as:
- Original papers.
- Official framework or vendor documentation.
- Established textbooks or university material.

Extract the mechanics, neighboring concepts, typical failure modes, and important tradeoffs.

## 3. Search interview and system-design patterns

Search current interview-question and ML system-design material for the topic or its nearest practical category.

Extract patterns such as:
- What data would you use?
- What would you build first?
- How would you evaluate it?
- What changes when labels are scarce or noisy?
- How would you prevent leakage?
- What happens under domain shift?
- What would you deploy under latency or cost limits?
- When is the simpler method the better engineering choice?

Do not copy question wording. Convert the reasoning pattern into a new scenario.

## 4. Create a reasoning map

Before writing questions, list at least 10 candidate reasoning dimensions and choose 10 distinct ones that fit the tier.

For each chosen dimension, record privately:
- Scenario domain.
- Main constraint.
- Best answer strategy.
- Three near-miss strategies.
- Why each near-miss loses under one specific constraint.

If two questions have the same best-answer strategy and the same underlying reason, replace one before drafting.

## 5. Research freshness

For topics whose scope, platform details, or best practices may change, prefer current sources. Do not invent current objectives from memory when web research is available.
